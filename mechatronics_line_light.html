Light Line

A movement based multiplayer game where players become lines of light and fight for space by cutting opponents before time runs out.

Overview

Title: Light Line

Engine: OpenCV, Python

Type: Physical game

Time to design and develop: 6 weeks

Team size: 2

Description: Light Line transforms body movement into gameplay. Players move physically in space to generate glowing trails on a shared LED grid. By cutting opponents’ lines and stealing their length, players compete using movement, timing, and spatial awareness instead of controllers.

Core Gameplay

The Experience

There are no buttons.

No joysticks.

No screens to stare at. Your body is the controller. Every movement leaves behind visible light. Every turn changes the battlefield. Every mistake becomes part of the arena itself. The floor remembers where players have been.

The Controller:

Instead of holding a traditional game controller, players carry a lightweight glowing stick with a colored light at its tip.

The stick acts as both an identity marker and a physical extension of the player inside the game. The overhead camera tracks the colored tip in real time, allowing movement to directly generate glowing trails on the LED grid.

The LED Grid:

Light Line was built around a custom LED grid containing 1700 individually addressable LEDs, where every single LED was carefully mapped and accounted for through a nomenclature system custom developed for the project.

The grid was precisely spaced and balanced to create the illusion of a perfect square play surface with 18 LEDs on each side. Instead of compressing the layout to save LEDs or space, the play area was intentionally designed to feel comfortable and natural for player movement.

How It Works

Camera → Processing → Arduino → LED Grid

An overhead camera continuously watches the play space and tracks the movement of players in real time using OpenCV. Each player carries a glowing colored stick, allowing the system to easily identify their position and movement across the arena.

This movement data is sent into Processing, where the game logic runs. Here, the system calculates player positions, generates trails, detects collisions, and handles interactions such as cutting opponents’ lines and transferring health.

Once the gameplay data is processed, it is sent to the ESP32, which controls the physical LED grid. The ESP32 updates the LEDs instantly, causing glowing trails to appear beneath the players as they move.

Final Experience

Light Line is designed to make players feel physically present inside the game rather than controlling it from outside. Every movement leaves behind glowing trails of light, turning the floor into a constantly changing battlefield of territory, tension, and interaction.

There are no buttons or joysticks. Players attack, defend, escape, and compete entirely through body movement and spatial awareness. The glowing trails slowly begin feeling less like visuals and more like extensions of the players themselves.

  • The Four Es of Interaction

The Four Es framework became an important game design perspective during the development of Light Line. Instead of treating the controller as a separate object, we focused on making movement, space, and interaction feel naturally connected to the player’s body and behavior. This helped the experience feel more physical, intuitive, and emotionally engaging rather than mechanical or screen driven.

1. Embodied:
Players physically become the line of light through their movement and presence.

2. Embedded:
The glowing trails create the feeling of marking and occupying territory within shared physical space.

3. Enactive:
Gameplay emerges directly through movement, turning physical actions into strategy and interaction.

4. Extended:
The glowing stick and light trail function as extensions of the player’s body and movement.

Key Learnings

1. Hardware and Software are Deeply Connected :
Camera responsiveness, LED behavior, frame rate, and gameplay feel all directly affected each other. Small technical changes created major differences in player experience.

2. Iteration Leads to Better Design:
Most mechanics evolved naturally through testing, observation, and experimentation rather than being fully planned from the start.

3. Movement Can Become Gameplay:
The project showed how physical movement and spatial interaction alone can create engaging and meaningful multiplayer gameplay.

  • Future Scope

The Light Line system is designed as a reusable interactive platform where multiple games and experiences can be developed using the same LED grid and tracking system.

A research paper is also being developed around the project, focusing on embodied interaction, movement based gameplay, and physical multiplayer experiences through interactive LED environments.

Light Line became both a game and a foundation for exploring new forms of interactive play.
